## Synthesis: How Warsh Differs from Powell

Warsh is best characterized as a **"hawkish dove"** whose sharpest breaks from Powell
are on the *balance sheet*, the *inflation framework*, and *communications* — not
necessarily the near-term rate path. He is a longtime QE critic (resigned from the
Board in 2011 over QE2), calls quantitative easing "reverse Robin Hood," and insists
the ~$6.6–7T balance sheet should be shrunk aggressively ("if it mattered going up, it
matters coming down") — a direct repudiation of the large balance sheet Powell built.
He rejects Powell's signature 2020 Flexible Average Inflation Targeting (FAIT)
framework in favor of a strict 2% target and wants to overhaul inflation measurement,
framing "inflation is a choice" the Fed owns rather than a supply-shock accident. On
communication he is Powell's near-opposite: he wants to "talk less, say more," abandon
forward guidance, possibly scrap the dot plot, and avoid signaling that "boxes the Fed
in" — and he acted on it immediately at his first FOMC (June 16–17, 2026: a ~130-word
statement, no personal dot, dropped forward guidance, five review task forces). The
paradox versus Powell: Warsh is *dovish on the policy rate in 2024–26* (favoring cuts,
justified partly by an AI-productivity-disinflation thesis) while being *hawkish on the
balance sheet and on credibility*. He was Trump's pick and confirmed on the narrowest
modern margin (54–45), so independence-versus-political-pressure is his defining
scrutiny axis; markets fell on June 17, 2026 precisely because he signaled hawkish
independence rather than the cuts the administration wanted.

| Axis | Warsh | Contrast with Powell |
|---|---|---|
| Hawk vs dove | Balance-sheet hawk; rate dove (2024–26); credibility hawk | Powell more centrist/discretionary |
| Inflation | "Inflation is a choice"; rejects Phillips-curve "dogma"; rethink measurement | — |
| Balance sheet / QT | Shrink aggressively (~$7T→~$4T); QE = "reverse Robin Hood" | Powell ran COVID QE, large balance sheet — sharpest divide |
| Fed independence | "Strict" on monetary policy; evasive on Trump's Powell/Cook attacks | Powell defended independence under Trump attacks |
| 2020 FAIT framework | Critic; revert to strict 2% target | Direct repudiation of a Powell-era choice |
| Forward guidance / comms | Abandon it; "talk less, say more"; kill the dot plot | Powell maximized guidance/projections — most concrete style contrast |
| Regulation | Deregulatory; aligns with VC-Supervision Bowman | — |
